# 📖 Lesson Plan — Behavioral-STAR-Bank

> **Chain T — Interview Prep** | A prepared bank of STAR stories — situation, task, action, result — drawn from real work, ready for behavioral rounds.

## What This Project Is

Build a reusable bank of STAR stories from real work, covering the questions that actually get asked, with quantified results.

## Learning Objectives

By the end I can:

1. Structure any story as Situation, Task, Action, Result.
2. Quantify the result rather than asserting it went well.
3. Cover conflict, failure, leadership, ambiguity, and influence.
4. Frame a career change as an asset rather than a gap.
5. Own a failure honestly and say what changed afterward.
6. Answer concisely enough to invite a follow-up.

## Software You Will Use

- A document or notes app.
- A recorder, to hear how the answers actually sound.

## Build Order

1. List every real situation worth telling from your career.
2. Draft each in STAR form.
3. Quantify results wherever numbers exist.
4. Map stories to the standard question categories; fill gaps.
5. Record yourself answering; cut the rambling.
6. Rehearse until the structure is invisible.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Describing the situation at length and rushing the action.
- Saying 'we' so consistently that your own contribution vanishes.
- Choosing a fake weakness instead of a real failure.
- Answers so long the interviewer stops listening.
- Apologising for a non-traditional background instead of using it.

## Check Your Understanding

The quiz covers STAR structure, result quantification, and category coverage.

## Why This Matters (Industry Application)

Behavioral rounds carry real weight, especially at larger companies, and a career-changer has to make the
prior career an asset rather than a gap to explain. My operations background is a differentiator — but only
if I can tell it well.

## Reflection Questions

- Which story best demonstrates judgement rather than effort?
- What does your leasing career let you say that a traditional candidate cannot?
